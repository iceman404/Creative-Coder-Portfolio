Reading the race: both tiers solve the same brightness-constancy problem on the same frame pair. The base tier is classic Horn-Schunck: 16 Jacobi iterations of the global smoothness-plus-data functional at full resolution. The multi-scale tier builds a 4-level pyramid, warps the second frame by the upsampled flow, and runs 8 proximal iterations per level from coarse to fine — the standard coarse-to-fine trick that catches large motions the base tier misses. Quality is the photometric residual: the mean brightness difference |I₁(x) − I₂(x + u)| over all pixels, lower is better, and both tiers are scored by the same measure. Flow direction maps to hue, magnitude to brightness.

MPM material (velocity-shaded, restarts every ~20 s)

The loop is the real material point method, in four GPU passes per frame: particle-to-grid transfer (quadratic B-spline weights, fixed-point atomic scatter), grid update (gravity + boundary conditions), grid-to-particle gather with the APIC affine velocity (each particle carries a local velocity matrix so the material keeps its spin and shear), and position advection. 32,768 particles, 128×72 grid, dt = 1/60. Brightness maps to speed, hue to velocity direction.

Path-traced scene (converging as samples accumulate)

One path per pixel per frame: camera ray, diffuse bounce, then direct light sampling through a ReSTIR-style reservoir — eight candidate positions are drawn on the area light per pixel per frame, weighted by their geometry and visibility terms, and only the winning candidate's shadow ray is actually traced. The radiance accumulates progressively, so the image converges as the samples-per-pixel counter climbs; reset it to watch the estimator's variance from the start. Temporal and spatial reservoir reuse (the full ReSTIR algorithm) is the next rung on this ladder.

Procedural stand-in scene, orbiting camera, tile-binned alpha blending

The full 3D Gaussian Splatting pipeline, per frame: every splat is projected to a screen-space 2D Gaussian (view-transformed covariance through the perspective Jacobian), assigned a packed (tile, depth) key, ordered by a GPU bitonic sort over all keys, scattered into per-tile instance ranges through a prefix-summed histogram, and tile-binned alpha-blended front-to-back with the standard saturation early-exit. The stand-in scene is procedural (a trunk and canopy sampled into anisotropic splats); pretrained scenes load through the vault registry as the next rung.
